The one-paragraph summary

The platform is an AI-assisted internal audit workspace, organised into Programs (planning and running audits), Global (shared masters and reporting) and System (settings). Its centre of gravity is the SOX / ICFR engagement, which is unusually well built — a proper five-step control-testing workflow, a materiality rule set, and a seven-rule severity engine that shows its own working. The blocker is not the audit content, it is that moving between screens is unreliable and very slow: the sidebar highlights the new module but the content pane lags 10–25 seconds or never updates at all.

Creating a SOX engagement — the 5-step wizard

From Engagements → New Engagement. The wizard is type-driven: it opens as five steps (Type · Basics · Scope · Team & Timeline · Review), and choosing an engagement type re-tailors the rest.

Step 1 · Type. Four options, each with a one-line description of what the workflow becomes: SOX / ICFR ("SOX 404 / ICFR — scoping, materiality rules, design + operating…"), Compliance ("Framework-driven control testing"), Internal Audit ("Process audit aligned to RACM + SOPs"), Automation ("Continuous monitoring / reconciliation / MIS / forensic").

Selecting SOX / ICFR collapsed the wizard from five steps to three — Type · Basics · Review. The type genuinely reconfigures the flow rather than just tagging the record.

Step 2 · Basics. Engagement name (pre-suggested from the group — "Suggested from the group — edit if your team names them differently"), an auto-generated code ("edit if your team uses its own scheme"), Owner, optional description, and Group (Listed / Holding). Then Entities in scope of the group audit, which starts empty — "No entities yet — upload the org chart or add them by hand" — with Add entity and Org Chart upload. Continue stays disabled until the group structure is settled. Ticking "There are no separate entities" auto-fills the table with the holding company, relabels the section to "Entity in scope", and unblocks Continue.
Step 3 · Review. Summary before creation.

Creating an audit round — the 4-step wizard

From inside an engagement, SOX testing → New audit. This is where materiality is set, and it is the most important form in the product. Tested end to end. Creates successfully

Step 1 · Audit period. "The window this audit tests, and which pass of the year it is." From / To dates, plus a Round selector — Interim ("Tests the first part of the period, well before the year end"), Roll-forward, Year-end. Once both dates are set it confirms in words: "This interim audit covers 01 Aug 2026 – 31 Dec 2026."
Step 2 · Materiality & files. Two optional uploads first — Trial balance and General ledger (XLSX · CSV), marked "Attach them if this audit needs them — you can add them later instead." Then the Materiality rule, framed as "Set before testing starts — exceptions are measured against it."
  • Basis — a dropdown, e.g. "% of profit before tax", annotated "Profit-making company — the classic base, typically ~5%."
  • Benchmark amount (₹ Cr) and Basis %.
  • Performance materiality as a % of overall — "auditors typically set 50–75%."
  • Clearly-trivial threshold as a % of overall — "below this, differences are passed."

The computed thresholds and severity ladder update live, and the arithmetic is correct. Entering ₹420 Cr profit before tax at 5%, PM 75%, CTT 5% produced:

Captioned "Set on Materiality & scope, shown here so you can see the effect before the audit is created." You see the consequence of the numbers before you commit to them.

Step 3 · Scope. "Scope by entity or by RACM — one or the other, then pick as many as the audit covers." A By entity / By RACM toggle, then a coverage meter reading 95.9% of the group covered · target 60% with the target marked on the bar and the note "Enough of the group is covered. Toggle any company in or out to overrule this."

Each company is scoped automatically by comparing its balance against performance materiality, and the reason is printed on every row — "₹648.50 Cr clears performance materiality" (in) versus "₹8.00 Cr is below ₹15.75 Cr" (out). Subsidiaries are indented under their parent, and a warning flags a structural gap: "1 company is held by a company that IS in scope, but left out: Altura Logistics Parks Pvt Ltd." Footer counts entities in scope.

Step 4 · Review. A summary table — Period, Round, Materiality (with its basis), Performance materiality, Clearly trivial, TB / GL attachment status, and the named entities — then Create audit.

On creation the audit appears in the SOX testing list and opens into its own Dashboard with every control reset to untested (0/45 TOD, 0/45 TOE) while open deficiencies carry forward — 4 open arrived on the new round. That is roll-forward behaving correctly: archive the concluded work, carry the unfinished.

The control dossier — the five-step testing workflow

Opening a control gives the working paper. The header carries chips (Key control, Financial, Manual, Preventive, Monthly, High risk, code), the control objective in plain English, the control activity narrative, and the identity line — Entity, Process, Control owner, Process owner, Risk, Assertions. Then OVERALL STATUS with a pressed stamp (INEFFECTIVE) and the two tracks: TOD Effective → TOE Ineffective. Buttons: Working paper, Reopen. Note: "Every run is logged in History."

1 · TOD — Test of Design. "The documents on file, one transaction traced end-to-end, and a design check for each thing that has to be true." Design elements are listed with REQUIRED/OPTIONAL tags and their status (Evidenced), each showing the attached file and who provided it. An Ira read this control panel suggests considerations worth testing that are not yet on the list, each with Add / dismiss — nothing is auto-inserted. The conclude footer says Evidence suggests Effective, pre-drafts the rationale ("All 2 design checks passed against the process narrative, flowchart and walkthrough on file"), and offers Conclude effective / Conclude ineffective.
2 · Population. "Pick the source file and filter it down to this control's instances, then check the count, the period and the source before locking it. Nothing downstream runs until it is locked." Live example: Locked · 2,000 instances from 14,000 rows, version POP-INT, source population_full_period.xlsx — system export (recorded at upload), filter shown, locked by A. Mehta. Includes a Preview and an IPE test — "the report this population came out of, proven before anything is built on it."
3 · Sample. "Drawn off the locked population, sized by how often the control runs, with the selection method and its seed stored so anyone can reproduce the same items." Live: 25 items — "Standard sample — moderate reliance. Drawn from POP-INT · 2,000 records · Agreed to GL control account."
4 · TOE — Test of Operating Effectiveness. Each attribute carries its assertion line and two independent evidence sections. Validation toggles between AI validation (Ask IRA) and Workflow, and requires a file first — the Run button is disabled and relabelled "Upload the required file first", with the warning "Upload the file the AI should check before running validation." Self-attestation is a separate toggle, default off. The conclude footer read Evidence suggests Ineffective with the drafted rationale "1 of 2 attributes passed across 25 sampled items. 1 failed."
5 · Sign-off. "The auditor signs the paper, the reviewer countersigns it, and the control is done. Nobody countersigns work they prepared." Two rows — Prepared by (A. Mehta) and Countersigned (J. Fernandes) — each showing not-yet-signed, and a Sign off button.

The right rail carries the three per-control confidence scores, live: Control completeness 100% (2/2 required elements evidenced — Complete), Evidence validated 0% (0/50 operating checks run — Needs attention, amber), and TOD coverage confidence 100% (2/2 considerations pass — Complete). Below them, tabs for History and Discussion, filterable by All / TOD / TOE.

When a control concludes ineffective, a Deficiency raised banner opens on the same page — no navigation away from the work.

Severity inputs — recomputed live against the ground rules

  • Likelihood — Remote / Reasonably possible / Probable.
  • Exposure ₹ — with a live comparison to materiality, and the definition stated on screen: "What could have slipped through while the control was broken — not the error actually found."
  • MW indicators — five fixed options (restatement, misstatement found by audit, senior-management fraud, ineffective control environment, ineffective period-end reporting).
  • Compensating control — a dropdown, annotated "capping Material Weakness → Significant Deficiency — never clears the exception."
  • Aggregation — derived chips (process · Treasury, assertion · Accuracy) marked "derived — not asked", plus an optional manual link for a shared root cause.
  • Prudent official — here reading "already at the top of the ladder — nothing to raise."

The severity engine shows its own working — and it is correct. Clicking "Show working" on DEF-A-01 printed all seven rules in order:

  1. MW indicator — none recorded.
  2. Compensating control — TRY-02 is tested effective, so it can cap a material weakness down to significant, and never clears the exception.
  3. Clearly trivial — ₹16.80 Cr is above the ₹60.0 L floor.
  4. Likelihood — Probable, the ladder applies.
  5. Exposure ladder — ₹16.80 Cr is at or above materiality ₹12.00 Cr ⇒ Material Weakness.
  6. Compensating control applied — capped from Material Weakness to Significant Deficiency by TRY-02. The exception stands.
  7. Aggregation — combines with 3 other exceptions sharing assertion — ₹36.40 Cr together ⇒ Material Weakness.
  8. Prudent official — no judgment applied.

So the cap genuinely reduced it, and aggregation then raised it back. The conclusion is not asserted — it is derived, and every step is on the record. This is the strongest single feature in the platform.

The step ends with a routing action — "Rated Material Weakness — send to the reviewer" — because anything graded significant or worse must have its rating confirmed before remediation begins.

Part 2 — The rest of the platform

Home

A configurable landing dashboard with ~14 widgets: headline counters, a "Set up your workspace" checklist, items waiting on you, a filterable platform activity feed, a quarterly health snapshot (completion vs target, risk counts, control effectiveness, compliance score with 8-quarter trend, framework coverage across SOX / ITGC / IFC), next-audit countdown, workflow performance, active engagements, open exceptions, data sources, shared items, recent queries, business processes, recent reports, AI Concierge shortcuts and a calendar. Layout is customisable.

Ask IRA

The AI copilot. You ask a question and it runs a short clarifying interview (up to four multiple-choice questions, each with a free-text escape), then executes while showing a right-hand panel with three tabs — Plan (a five-step execution plan naming the datasets it touched), Code and Sources. It can ask a further question mid-run, and finishes with a titled result: headline statistics, charts and a table. An editable Assumptions strip records what defaults were applied.

Caveat. The clarifying questions do not follow the question asked — "Summarize this quarter's exceptions" produced four questions about duplicate-invoice detection and a duplicate-payment analysis. The machinery is real; the routing from question to analysis is not.

Audit Planning

A Gantt timeline of engagements grouped by process, with completion percentages and filters on process and status. Plan Engagement opens a drawer capturing name, audit type, framework, primary process, audit period, planned dates, owner, reviewer, RACM version and description — with a read-only SOX Enforcement panel explaining that reviewer approval, evidence requirements, key-control validation and period locking will be enforced, and that enforcement follows the framework rather than the audit type. Tested and works — an engagement was created and appeared immediately on the timeline.

Engagements

The portfolio: tiles for Total 18 / Active 8 / Portfolio health 80% / Open findings 33, then a three-way breakdown by type (SOX / ICFR 6, Compliance 3, Internal Audit 4, Automation 5), by status and by process — every row clickable into a filtered library. Plus Needs attention and Upcoming milestones. Tabs: Overview, All Engagements, Approval Flow.

Process Hub

Coverage per business process with risk, control and RACM counts — P2P 67%, O2C 57%, R2R 50%, S2C 0%.

Dashboard

Analytics dashboards (separate from Home) with My Dashboards / Shared with Me, search and Create Dashboard. Each card badges its data source — Excel/CSV, SQL or Query — so you can see at a glance whether it is live-queried or file-based.

Risk Register

The master risk list. Each row carries an ID, title, process, category, severity, description and its mapped control chips, with filters on priority, sub-process and category. A banner warns that 3 risks are not yet mapped to controls. AI insights are explicitly human-gated — the panel states it "won't run automatically; you trigger it so it only bills when you need it."

What is good, and what holds it back

Genuinely good

  • The SOX testing model is right. Design gates operating, the population must be locked before a sample is drawn, the seed is stored for reproducibility, and nobody countersigns their own paper. These are real audit disciplines, enforced by the product rather than left to the user.
  • Severity is derived, not typed — and the engine prints its reasoning step by step.
  • AI is gated and attributed. Ask IRA shows plan, code and sources; the risk register makes you trigger the expensive step; Ira suggests design checks but never inserts them.
  • Roles are first-class. Auditor / Risk Owner / Reviewer switching, "whose court" badges, and a court column on the deficiency register.
  • Findings are surfaced, not buried — unmapped risks, open material weaknesses and the year-end countdown all push themselves to the top.

Holding it back

  • Navigation is unreliable and slow — the single biggest issue.
  • Nothing persists — a reload discarded the engagement created minutes earlier.
  • The URL never changes, so nothing can be bookmarked, shared or reached with the back button.
  • Numbers disagree between screens — Home says 1 active engagement, Engagements says 8.
  • Uploads cannot be completed in a browser-automation context because the buttons open a native OS file picker.

If only one thing gets fixed: module navigation. The SOX engagement underneath it is strong enough to sell the product — but nobody will reach it while a sidebar click takes twenty seconds or silently does nothing.
