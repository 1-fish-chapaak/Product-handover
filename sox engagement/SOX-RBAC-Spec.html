Role Based Access Control

SOX / ICFR engagement

1 · The three roles

RoleWhat they do
AuditorDoes the testing and decides whether a control works
Control ownerRuns the control day to day, and fixes it when it breaks
ReviewerChecks the auditor's work and signs it off

The same person can own a control in two ways, as the owner of the control itself or as the owner of the process around it. Either one counts as owning it.

A problem found during testing is called a deficiency. The file holding all the evidence for one control is its audit file.

Control owner

CanCannot

The fix plan can be edited only while it is still with the owner. Once it goes back to the auditor, it is frozen.

Reviewer

Reads everything and signs. Cannot change anything in the testing.

3 · What each role sees

RoleTabs inside an auditWhere they land
AuditorDashboard, Control Library, Deficiency management, ConfigurationThe dashboard
ReviewerThe same fourThe dashboard, plus a list of what is waiting on them
OwnerThree only: Overview, Control Library, their own deficienciesA task list, not a dashboard

The owner keeps a deficiency view on purpose. Writing the plan and doing the fix are their jobs, and someone who cannot reach their own work cannot do it. The list only shows their controls.

What the owner is not shown

ScreenWhat the owner gets instead
Control LibraryOnly controls in their name, and the count says so
A controlTheir documents and what is still outstanding, then a line saying the testing and its result are not shown here. The later steps are simply absent
History of actionsTheir own actions, plus document requests and what they sent back
The audit fileA short summary of what they need to fix, built only from what they are allowed to see
How many items get tested, and whyNothing. That is the audit team's method

The rule the product follows, and the right one: whatever a role cannot do is not on the screen at all, rather than greyed out.

4 · Locks that stop everyone

Two locks have nothing to do with roles. They stop everybody, the auditor included, so that a signed decision cannot quietly change afterwards.

LockWhen it appliesHow it opens
Control lockThe moment a control is decidedThe auditor unlocks it with a reason, or the reviewer sends it back with a reason
Audit lockThe moment the audit is signed off by the reviewerIt does not. This one is permanent

Both are checked at the point something is saved, so they hold whichever screen the change came from.

5 · Who holds a deficiency, and when

Work passes back and forth so that no two steps in a row sit with the same person.

StageWithDoing
FoundAuditorWriting down what went wrong, then judging how serious it is
Rating checkReviewerAgreeing how serious it is, before any fix starts
PlanningOwnerWriting the fix plan, or rewriting a rejected one
Plan checkAuditorChecking the plan actually addresses the cause
FixingOwnerDoing the work and uploading proof
Testing the fixAuditorTesting again on fresh items
Final checkReviewerReading the proof and closing it
ClosedNobodyThe auditor or the reviewer can reopen it by giving a reason

The rating check blocks everything. Nothing else can happen until the reviewer agrees or sends it back, because a plan written for the wrong level of seriousness is wasted work.

A written challenge moves the work too. While one is open the control sits with the auditor, and once answered it sits with the reviewer.

The screen names the person holding it, not the role. You cannot chase a role for an answer.
