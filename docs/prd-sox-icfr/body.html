SOX / ICFR Engagement Management

Overview

Executive summary & contents

From the BRD. SOX / ICFR becomes a first-class engagement type on the platform — created, scoped, and managed alongside every other engagement, not as a separate tool. An audit team can run multiple SOX engagements in parallel, each with its own scope, materiality ground rules, control set, testing, and exception register, under one consistent methodology.

Why now (from the BRD)

Previously SOX / ICFR lived as a standalone section — which blocked creating more than one SOX engagement and kept it outside the engagement portfolio. This release makes SOX / ICFR selectable in the Create Engagement flow and lists it in the Engagement Library with its own type, so it inherits the platform's portfolio, roles, and governance — while opening into a dedicated SOX testing workspace.

Where this PRD stands. The feature is being built from scratch — it does not exist in the production product today. The BRD defines the business requirement; the working prototype demonstrates the full target experience; this PRD is the build contract between the two. The basics — problem, purpose, scope, personas, journey, materiality rules, exception lifecycle — come from the BRD. Where the prototype goes beyond the BRD, the addition is highlighted in an amber block like the one below, so reviewers can approve the baseline and the additions separately.

Beyond the BRD — what the prototype adds (each highlighted in place)
  • An Overview dashboard with a year-end checklist that ends in an engagement-level sign-off (preparer signs, reviewer countersigns) — the BRD's sign-off stops at closing individual exceptions (§5.2).
  • Three new tabs: RACM as a pre-testing review surface, Risk Library (inherent ⇄ residual heatmaps), and Test runs (a log of every execution) (§5.4, §5.5, §5.10).
  • Review notes — the reviewer's tracked challenges that block a paper's countersign until resolved and verified (§5.7, §5.13).
  • Per-paper sign-off — each control's working paper is signed by the preparer and countersigned by the reviewer, with a preview that equals the export (§5.9).
  • IPE validation gate — a system report must be vouched for (three checks) before a sample can be drawn from it (§5.8).
  • Person-based four-eyes — the same human can never countersign their own paper or close a retest they recorded, regardless of role (§2, §8).
  • Sealed ground rules — mid-engagement threshold changes demand a reason, preview their regrades, and land in a visible change history (§5.12).
  • Safety furniture — attest confirms on every irreversible act, evidence-gated retest submission, a reopen-with-reason path, sample-size guidance with extend-on-fail, the To-do bell, and a person-scoped owner portal (§5.3, §5.11, §5.14, §8).

Section 1

Problem, purpose & scope

1.1 Problem statement (from the BRD)

The platform has no SOX / ICFR capability an audit team can actually run a programme on. What exists today is a standalone section — and that shape is the problem the BRD names:

The gap this release closes
  1. Only one SOX engagement can exist. A standalone screen cannot hold a second engagement — yet a real audit team runs several ICFR programmes in parallel (the prototype seeds three: P2P/O2C, O2C, R2R).
  2. SOX sits outside the engagement portfolio. It is invisible to the Engagement Library's health, findings, milestones and governance — leadership cannot see SOX beside Compliance, Internal Audit and Automation.
  3. It inherits none of the platform's roles. Owner / reviewer chains, whose-court hand-offs, and role-scoped views exist platform-wide but never applied to SOX.
  4. No methodology enforcement. Materiality, the severity ladder, the TOD-before-TOE order, and the exception lifecycle live in the team's heads and spreadsheets — nothing on the platform enforces them.

The BRD's answer: make SOX / ICFR a first-class engagement type — created from the same flow, listed in the same portfolio, governed by the same roles — that opens into a dedicated SOX testing workspace enforcing the methodology end to end.

1.2 Purpose of this document (from the BRD)

As the BRD puts it: specify the target experience and business rules for running a SOX 404(b) / ICFR engagement on the platform — so client stakeholders can review the flow, the materiality rule set, and the exception lifecycle before and during implementation. This PRD carries that to build depth — screens, rules, gates, use cases and edge cases — in plain English, so implementation and client stakeholders share one testable definition of done. The feature is a from-scratch build; the prototype in this document is the reference implementation of the target.

1.3 In scope (from the BRD)

Engagement creation and typing; entity & account scoping; the materiality ground-rule set; the control register; Test of Design (TOD) and Test of Operating Effectiveness (TOE) with evidence; deficiency evaluation against materiality; and the exception lifecycle through to close and sign-off.

The prototype specifies additional surfaces on top of this baseline — the Overview dashboard and engagement sign-off, RACM, Risk Library, Test runs, review notes, the working-paper sign-off chain, IPE validation, and the To-do bell. Each is marked "Beyond the BRD" where it appears.

Section 2

Personas & roles

Auditor Owns the testing. Scopes controls, sets the materiality ground rules, runs Test of Design and Test of Operating Effectiveness, validates the reports testing relies on, concludes each control, evaluates how bad each exception is, signs the working papers as preparer, and signs the engagement first.
Reviewer The independent second pair of eyes. Works a queue of papers awaiting countersign, raises review notes (tracked challenges the auditor must resolve before a paper can close), verifies resolutions, closes exceptions after a passed retest, and countersigns the engagement to conclude it.
Risk / Control Owner First line — the person who runs the control day to day. Sees a scoped to-do, never the whole engagement: provide requested documents, attest & evidence due control tests, and execute remediation plans when something breaks.
Four-eyes is person-based, not just role-based. The product tracks who did each act. The same human cannot countersign a paper they prepared or close a retest they recorded — even wearing the reviewer hat. Every hand-off is explicit: each control shows whose court the work is in.

The workspace top bar carries a "Viewing as" switch (a demo affordance for walking the three views). It is offered at engagement level only — inside a control page the persona is fixed; go back to the engagement to switch hats.

3.1 Auditor — A. Mehta

When…The product…
She opens the engagement on a Monday and needs to know what stands between here and the opinion date.The Overview's year-end box lists exactly what must close — open exceptions, unconcluded controls, papers awaiting sign-off — each line clickable to the work itself, with the sign-off as the final step of the same list (§5.2).
She needs documents from the business to test a control's design.One click raises a document request; it lands on the owner's to-do and the bell, and the control shows "waiting on owner" until it comes back (§5.7).
She is about to sample vendor changes from a system report.The Draw button stays locked until she validates the report itself (IPE): a three-check attestation — completeness, accuracy, report parameters — stamped under her name (§5.8).
She wants to test many controls in one sitting.Bulk test: pick the scope, attach the datasets once, execute — with a Back that keeps her attachments and a Stop that commits nothing (§5.6).
A test fails.The product never lets a failure be "small miss, ignore": it prompts to extend the sample, and an ineffective conclusion raises an exception sized against the ground rules (§5.11).
All testing is done and she signs off.Signing is a two-step attest ("You can't un-sign"), and if a material weakness is open the button says plainly that signing concludes ICFR not effective (§5.2).

3.2 Risk / Control Owner — M. Nair

When…The product…
He logs in wondering what is on him this week.His Overview is a single checklist — control tests due (his move: attest & evidence), document requests, remediations — each row dated, overdue in red, one click into the control (§5.14).
A control test is due on a control he runs.He opens it, attaches evidence, writes how the attribute is satisfied, and attests pass or fail under his own name. He never runs audit tests — the wording says attest & evidence, never TOD/TOE.
A control he owns was concluded ineffective.The bell tells him what happened and what is next ("open to plan remediation"); the exception carries a remediation plan with his name and a real due date; overdue escalates visibly (§5.11).
The fix is done."Fixed — submit for retest" is blocked until evidence of the fix is attached — "done" needs proof. The exception moves to the auditor for retest.

3.3 Reviewer — J. Fernandes

When…The product…
She opens the engagement to see what waits on her.Her desk lists exactly four kinds of work: concluded papers to countersign, resolved notes to verify, exceptions whose retest passed, and — once the preparer signs — the engagement countersign (§5.13).
The testing on a paper does not satisfy her.She raises a review note — a tracked challenge. The paper cannot be countersigned while any note is open; the auditor resolves with a response, she verifies and closes the note.
A remediated exception passed its retest.Closing asks her to confirm — it is the terminal four-eyes act — and if she herself recorded that retest, the product refuses: a different person must close it.
Something was closed too early.Auditor or reviewer can reopen a closed exception with a recorded reason; it returns to remediation and the stale retest clears (§7).

5.1 — Engagement Library: the portfolio & creating an engagement

SOX / ICFR engagements live in the same library as everything else — filter the list by type and the three ICFR engagements appear with their health and open findings.

Engagements → Engagement Library → All Engagements (filtered to SOX / ICFR)

Engagement Library — the SOX / ICFR filter shows FY26 ICFR (75% effective, 3 open), O2C (89%), and a planned R2R engagement.

Behaviour & rules
  1. One portfolio — SOX engagements list beside Compliance, Internal Audit and Automation, each with a health bar (controls effective) and open-findings count.
  2. Type filter — the "SOX / ICFR" filter narrows the list; each card carries code, owner, period, process and framework tags.
  3. Entry — clicking a card opens that engagement's SOX workspace (§5.2); New Engagement opens the four-step creation flow below.

Creating a SOX engagement — the four-step flow

New Engagement opens a drawer over the library — the same flow for every engagement type, which is the BRD's point: SOX is created like everything else, not in a separate tool. Step 2 changes shape with the chosen type; for SOX / ICFR it is the materiality ground rules. The progress bar is clickable backwards; a step ahead stays locked until the current one passes its checks.

Engagement Library → New Engagement → Step 1 · Type & basics

Step 1 — Type & basics: pick SOX / ICFR, name the engagement, set entity, process and period. Next stays off until type, name, code and a valid period (end after start) are in.

New Engagement → Step 2 · Scope (SOX / ICFR)

Step 2 — Scope: the three thresholds, with the severity ladder computed live beneath as the numbers change — the same ladder that will grade every exception in testing (§5.12, §6).

The four steps & their gates
  1. Type & basics — engagement type · name · auto-generated code (editable if the team uses its own scheme) · entity · process · period · owner. Gate: type, name and code present; period end after start.
  2. Scope (SOX / ICFR) — the framework is fixed to COSO 2013 / SOX 404(b); a key-controls-only toggle (default on); overall materiality, with performance materiality and clearly-trivial as % of overall (defaults 75% / 5%; the significant-deficiency band sits at 20%). These are set once here and sealed — changing them later goes through the reasoned, previewed edit of §5.12. Draft with AI can prefill the step from an uploaded engagement letter or prior-year scope; the result is banner-flagged "Drafted by AI — review before continuing".
  3. Team & timeline — reviewer (required, and must differ from the owner — four-eyes starts at creation), auditors, risk owners; 2–4 dated milestones prefilled from the period (Kickoff · Fieldwork complete · Sign-off) that feed the portfolio timeline.
  4. Review — everything read back in collapsible sections. Create as Draft parks it in the library; Create & Activate opens it live. Either way, the full configuration is carried onto the engagement.
Deviations from the BRD in the creation flow
  • The wizard is 4 steps, not 5 — Type → Scope → Team & timeline → Review (the BRD's captures show Type / Basics / Details / Team / Review).
  • The sign-off chain is preparer → reviewer — the BRD's "audit committee" third step was dropped by decision; the engagement concludes at the reviewer's countersign.
  • Opening an engagement lands on Overview (§5.2), a dashboard — the BRD lands directly on the control register, which is one tab over.

5.3 — The To-do bell

The bell is a live worklist, not a notification archive: it recomputes from the engagement, so an item disappears the moment its work is done. Each persona sees their own pending list, worded in their own language.

Top bar → To-do (Auditor view)

To-do — pending assignment & review for the persona in view; every row is a deep link into the exact control.

Behaviour & rules
  1. Per-persona — the auditor sees rows awaiting review and open exceptions; the owner sees due tests, requests and verdicts on their controls; the reviewer sees what waits on their pen.
  2. Event + next step — every row pairs what happened with what to do ("concluded INEFFECTIVE… open to plan remediation"). Owner-facing wording says attest & evidence — never audit jargon.
  3. Urgency first — ineffective verdicts rank at the top with a count badge on the bell.
Beyond the BRD

The To-do bell is entirely new — the BRD has no notification surface. It exists because the whose-court hand-offs the BRD asks for need a place where each person can see what waits on them.

5.5 — Risk Library

Two heatmaps tell the engagement's risk story at a glance: inherent (before controls) beside residual (after control testing) — how far testing has actually pulled exposure down.

SOX workspace → Risk Library

Risk Library — Impact × Likelihood heatmaps (labelled axes, click any cell to filter), and the register beneath with "n/m effective" controls per risk.

Behaviour & rules
  1. Two filter layers — the toolbar above the maps (search · process · status · an Identified between date window, bounded to the engagement period and defaulting to all of it) scopes both heatmaps and the register together; clicking a heatmap cell then narrows the register alone to that Likelihood × Impact, shown as a clearable chip. The count line stays honest: "Showing n of m risks".
  2. Register row — risk ID · description (full text on hover) · process · inherent and residual scores · "↓ reduced by controls" where testing moved the number · controls as effective / total · status.
  3. Drill-through — a risk opens its mapped controls; a control opens its page (§5.7).

The idea, in one line: inherent is the risk's nature — how often it would bite and how hard if no control existed at all — so it never moves during the year. Residual is what remains after controls, and the product takes a deliberate stance on it: a control that merely exists on paper earns nothing; only a control proven effective in testing moves the number. That is why the two maps sit side by side — the left one never changes, the right one moves as testing progresses, and the gap between them is exactly what the testing effort has bought.

How the scores work
  1. One row per risk — every unique risk in the RACM becomes one register row, with the controls that answer it bundled onto it and the date it was identified on the row (this is what the date filter reads). The Risk Library has no data entry of its own; it reads the engagement.
  2. A score is Likelihood × Impact — each rated 1 to 5 (Rare → Almost certain, Minimal → Severe), so scores run 1–25. Every badge shows its working, e.g. 12 = L4 × I3.
  3. Inherent is set with the engagement — and a risk answered by a key control never rates below Moderate impact. Residual starts equal to inherent: a brand-new engagement shows two identical maps, and they separate only as test conclusions land — by the table below.

How residual moves. Look at where the risk's mapped controls stand — that one fact fixes both the score movement and the status chip:

Worked example — one risk through the engagement
  1. R-04 enters the year at inherent L4 × I4 = 16 — Critical: a red cell on the left map, top of the register. Two controls answer it.
  2. The first control concludes effective → residual L3 × I4 = 12 — High. Status: In testing.
  3. The second concludes effective too → residual L2 × I3 = 6 — Moderate; the row now carries "↓ reduced by controls". Status: Mitigated.
  4. A later retest finds the second control failing → an exception opens and residual snaps straight back to 16. R-04 returns to the top of the register — and stays there until the retest passes and the exception closes.

The register sorts by residual score, highest first — the top of the list is always what still hurts.

5.8 — Control page: Operating effectiveness & IPE

Operating effectiveness asks: did the control actually work, all period long? The evidence chain is enforced in order: population → validate the report itself (IPE) → draw the sample → evidence each attribute → conclude.

Control page → ② Test of operating effectiveness

Operating effectiveness — the population is uploaded but "IPE not validated"; Draw stays locked until the report itself is vouched for.

Validate IPE → the three-check attestation

Validate IPE — completeness (the row count ties to the source), accuracy (rows traced back), report parameters (nothing silently excluded). "Validate & stamp" stays off until all three are ticked; the stamp records who and when.

Behaviour & rules
  1. IPE in one line — Information Produced by the Entity: any report the company's own system generated. A sample is only as good as the report it came from, so the report is validated first — and the trail records "validated the population (IPE) — completeness & accuracy".
  2. Sampling — the suggested sample size follows the control's frequency (the handbook table, §6); deviating is allowed and noted. A failed attribute prompts extend the sample — a failure is never "small miss, ignore".
  3. Attributes — each condition is evidenced independently: an automated check (AI validation with a required input file, or a mapped monitoring workflow) or the owner's self-attestation (note + evidence + pass/fail under their name). The attribute row names its conclusion of record; a stale machine verdict that disagrees is marked "superseded".
  4. Sample status — a compact "n/m tested · n fail" count (per-item chips are deliberately not shown at 25+ samples).
  5. Conclude — the footer recommends from the evidence; concluding against it demands a written rationale. Ineffective raises an exception and tells the owner what happens next.
Beyond the BRD

Per-attribute evidence (AI validation ⇄ workflow, separate self-attestation, bulk test, overrides) is BRD §3.6. 5.11 — Exceptions: the lifecycle

An ineffective control raises an exception. Each is sized against the ground rules and walks a five-stage lifecycle — Identified → Remediation → Retest → Awaiting reviewer → Closed — three lanes, three hats: the owner remediates, the auditor evaluates and retests, the reviewer closes.

Overview → Manage exceptions

Exceptions — DEF-001 in Remediation as a Significant Deficiency; the conclusion leads with the grade, the derivation sits behind "Show working"; the overdue plan is flagged "overdue — escalate".

Behaviour & rules
  1. Severity inputs, recomputed live — likelihood · exposure ₹ (what could have slipped through, not the error found) · MW indicator chips (full text on hover) · compensating control (every option named, not just a code) · aggregation group · a prudent-official "raise to MW" that only goes up.
  2. Conclusion reads as an answer — the final grade leads; "Show working" reveals the chain (capped from…, indicator forces…). A compensating control can cap — never clear — a deficiency, and never argues down an MW indicator.
  3. Remediation — action, owner, a real date picker for the due date; overdue escalates visibly; "submit for retest" requires attached proof of the fix.
  4. Retest & close — pass hands it to the reviewer; fail loops to remediation. Closing is an attest confirm under the reviewer's name — and a different person than whoever recorded the retest. A closed exception can be reopened with a recorded reason.
  5. Auto-routing — when the policy is on, a fresh exception lands directly in the owner's remediation lane and the trail says so.
Beyond the BRD

The lifecycle, computed severity, compensating-control cap and aggregation are BRD §3.8. New here: the explicit Awaiting reviewer stage (the BRD folds it into "Closed"), evidence-gated retest submission, the attest-confirmed four-eyes close, reopen-with-reason, the grade-first "show working" presentation, and instant owner-lane routing when auto-routing is on.

Captured at creation, managed in Materiality & scope (§5.12), and applied to every exception (§5.11). In plain terms: materiality is the amount that would matter to someone reading the financial statements — every exception is sized against it.

Edge cases & guard-rails

The situations that break spreadsheet-run programmes — and what the product does in each. Every row below is implemented and walkable in the build.

SituationWhat the product does
Someone tries to test operating effectiveness before design is concluded.The ② section is visibly locked — "Conclude the Test of Design as effective to unlock". No way around it.
A sample is drawn from a report nobody has vouched for.Impossible — Draw stays disabled until the IPE attestation (three checks) is stamped.
The preparer tries to countersign their own paper.Refused with the reason: "You signed this paper — a different person must countersign it." Same rule for closing an exception whose retest you recorded.
The reviewer tries to countersign a paper with an open review note.The countersign is disabled and says why; the note must be resolved by the auditor and verified by the reviewer first.
A terminal act is one misclick away.Every irreversible act — engagement sign-off, countersign, exception close, roll-forward — passes an attest confirm that says what it concludes and that it can't be undone.
A material weakness is still open when the preparer signs.Signing is never blocked — but the confirm and the status say plainly: this concludes ICFR not effective. The stamped conclusion then wins over later recomputes.
Someone edits materiality mid-engagement to make an exception disappear.Threshold edits stage as a draft, demand a written reason, preview the regrades, and land in a visible change history. The grade moves with the rule — openly.
A compensating control is offered to soften a material weakness.It can cap MW → SD only if that control is itself concluded effective here; it never clears the exception; and it never argues down an MW indicator.
An attribute fails on a small sample.The product prompts to extend the sample — sizing the problem before concluding is the rule, ignoring it isn't offered.
An automated control relies on "test of one" while an ITGC has failed.The reliance is invalidated — the sample guidance recomputes and says why.
The owner marks a fix done without proof."Submit for retest" is blocked until evidence is attached — "done" needs proof.
An exception was closed too early.Auditor or reviewer reopens it with a recorded reason; it returns to Remediation and the stale retest clears.
A concluded, countersigned control needs rework.The paper is final and read-only; the only way back in is a reopen with a recorded reason — results stay, conclusions clear, the trail records why.
The engagement is concluded and someone keeps editing.The engagement locks: testing, rules and exceptions are read-only; the Overview box collapses to the signed summary.
The owner lands on an audit-side page (deep link, role switch).Owner mode simply doesn't render engagement-wide surfaces — their tabs are Overview and their own controls; materiality, sign-off, RACM, risks and runs are absent. Owner-facing severity views hide the engagement's thresholds.
Roll-forward to year-end is clicked by accident.A confirm explains it is one-way (design carries; manual and IT-dependent operating tests reset; automated keep results) before anything happens.
Half a new-control form is typed, then Escape.A discard guard asks first; nothing is lost silently.
A role switch happens deep inside a control.It can't — the persona is fixed inside the control page; the switcher lives at engagement level only.
